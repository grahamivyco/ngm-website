Thistle Threads

Tricia Wilson Nguyen

Silk and goldOne three-day class

Patricia Wilson Nguyen is an independent researcher, professional embroiderer, and founder of Thistle Threads, specializing in the experimental archaeology of historic embroidered textiles. Her research on the pedagogical infrastructure of early modern needlework includes “On Female Education: Projects of Mastery in Seventeenth-Century English Boarding Schools” (Winterthur Portfolio, 2025). She co-authored works on materiality and making in Textile History (2025/2026).

She led the Plimoth Jacket Project, a reconstruction exhibited at Winterthur, manufactures reproduction threads, and taught a 600-page course on reticella. She holds a Ph.D. in Materials Science and Engineering from the University of Michigan and an S.B. from MIT.

Silken Alchemy

Transforming goldwork with color

We know the gleam of gold thread embroidery from church vestments and royal garments: couched gold in patterns, padded textures, shimmering purls, checks, and bullions. But what happens when we replace those traditional metallics with silk versions?

In this creative 3-day workshop, we’ll explore just that, reimagining historic goldwork techniques using silk gimps, purls, and checks. These silk thread versions, rarely used in this way, bring vibrant color and a new kind of texture to traditional embroidery methods, opening the door to exciting new effects and combinations.

Each day includes a focused lecture on core techniques and their historical context, followed by extended hands-on sessions for experimentation. You’ll play with layering, padding, filling, and mixing silk constructions with metallics, discovering how contrast, color, and texture work together in fresh ways. Think of this as a playground for stitchers with an experimental spirit.

To support your creative freedom, each kit includes a curated assortment of unusual silk threads: colorful gimps from a happy dyeing accident, and purls and checks in various sizes left over from kit preparation. These “bonus” materials are perfect for guilt-free exploration.

Bring your curiosity, imagination, and any odd bits of silk gimp, purls, or gold threads you have tucked away. This is your chance to turn forgotten scraps into bold new ideas. Whether you’re expanding your goldwork skills or looking to innovate within tradition, Silken Alchemy offers a unique space to play, learn, and invent.

Guild member

Lee Popp

Queen stitchesOne half-day class

Long-time Guild member Lee Popp started stitching at age 9 and hasn’t quit since: 73 years of some type of needle in her hand. She fell in love with the much-maligned queen stitch when she happened upon an EGA article with instructions for an easier way to execute this complex stitch. She would love to teach it to you, with the hope that you may also learn to like, if not love, the Queen of all stitches.

  • Half-day

The Queen of All Stitches

The year is 1666, and thou art granted an audience with the queen… yea, the sovereign of all stitches. Mark me, this is no manner of monarch whom thou hast grown to loathe for her harsh and prickly nature. Nay, this queen is of a far sweeter disposition, passing gentle and kind in her ways. In truth, to know her is to hold her in most wondrous love.

Translation: we will learn an easier, faster, and better way to do queen stitches, using a pattern published in 1666.

Summer House Stitche Workes

Beth Seal

Counted threadFour one-day classes

Beth Ann Seal of Summer House Stitche Workes grew up in rural Nittany, PA. She first learned to embroider as a child and learned counted cross stitch from a cousin while in high school. Her early employment at Woolrich, Inc. included several positions from sewer to industrial engineer technician. Working with new garment designs gave her a well-rounded education in sewing techniques and construction.

After a tenure as a Naval Officer’s wife, and once her three children were all in school, she returned to the classroom as well, earning a BS in graphic design from Pennsylvania College of Technology. Since then, she has worked for a professional photographer and created thousands of invitations as the in-house designer for Bella Figura, a luxury stationer. In 2006, she started Summer House Stitche Workes and now eats, sleeps and breathes needlework. Beth enjoys spreading her love of the needle through teaching and meeting stitchers across the country.

Dance of the Wildflowers

Mattress pinkeep

Let your needle take a hop, skip and a jump through a wildflower meadow as you create this mattress-finish pin cushion. The side panel combines cross stitches and embroidery stitches to create a colorful blanket of flowers teeming with fluttering butterflies and jumping rabbits. The flowers take on a three-dimensional effect with the addition of Dorset buttons. A blooming flower created from a wool-blend felt piece and embroidery stitches is the proverbial icing on the top of the pin cushion.

Stitches on the meadow panel include cross stitch, backstitch, fly stitch, French knot, Algerian eye stitch, lazy daisy stitch, and spider web stitch. Stitched on 32 ct. Country French Cafe Mocha from Wichelt with floss from Weeks Dye Works.

  • One day

Woodland Waltz

Quartet of strawberries

Breathe deeply of the crisp autumnal air as brilliant leaves fall around you. Picture yourself taking a walk down a wooded path and you will experience the inspiration behind this quartet of strawberries.

The larger strawberries are stitched on 32 count Latte linen from Fiber on a Whim with hand-dyed floss from Weeks Dye Works and Classic Colorworks, then embellished with wool felt leaves, vintage buttons and seasonal charms. The smaller strawberries, made from quilting cotton and filled with emery, are the perfect companions. A little accompanying sampler rounds out the stitching projects for this class. Autumn 3 Ways

Maple leaves are done three ways in this new design. You will have a choice of many different autumn-colored fabrics to fabric-appliqué your first leaf. The middle leaf is executed with a beautiful shaded laid filling pattern in autumn colors. Needle felting right onto the canvas with autumn-colored rayon and wool roving completes your last leaf. Embroidered stems and veins finish each leaf.

Five borders frame the leaves, with several different stitches picking up all the colors in the leaves. Stitched on eggshell Congress cloth, the design finishes to 10″ × 10″. The stitches use many fun threads, including Painters Threads Overdyed Floss, Planet Earth Silk Veggies, DMC and Anchor floss, Rainbow Gallery Sparkle! Braid and Splendor silk, and Soy Luster Shadows.
